# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Young adults, roughly 18 to 30, anywhere in the world, who want to change how they look and live. They are motivated by dating, by fitness, by adventure, by wanting to walk into a room differently. They are not athletes and not patients. They have absorbed years of contradictory advice from social media and have no accurate picture of their own starting point.

They are used to games and they are suspicious of being sold to. Both of those matter.

Most health tools are built on European or American reference data and quietly applied to everyone. A user can optionally give their ancestry and their country. Ancestry changes the thresholds that ancestry has been shown to change. Country changes food and access. Neither changes anything else, and the product says so where it changes nothing.

## Product Purpose

Every product aimed at this person is paid for by their insecurity. Apps sell subscriptions, coaches sell plans, supplement brands sell powder, and creators sell the body they were born with. A product whose revenue depends on you feeling short of the mark cannot afford to tell you that you are closer than you think, or that the thing you want will take two years, or that the trait you are trying to fix is mostly fine.

fit-lab is free and sells nothing, so it can say all three.

You build a body from your own measurements, in 3D, and it changes as the tape does. You check it against a photo so it stays honest rather than flattering. Then the product tells you where you actually stand, and it means that literally: every number is placed against 71,543 real adults measured by the US national health survey. Not "your BMI is 26.4", which is noise, but "higher than 33% of men aged 25 to 29", which is the thing you actually wanted to know.

Then it tells you whether the goal in your head is reachable, how long it would honestly take, and the complete movement foundation, shown on real anatomy and arranged as a usable weekly checklist. The app chooses the coverage; the user chooses only whether variants should fit a defined-minimum home gym or a full commercial gym. Muscle-gain foundations stay full-body at two sessions, use Upper/Lower/Full body at three, and repeat Upper/Lower at four so every anatomy region is trained at least twice without changing the evidence-set weekly volume.

It is an assessment with a deterministic starting foundation, not an adaptive programme generator. It does not periodise blocks, prescribe load jumps, or pretend to coach recovery. The checklist makes the evidence-set starting dose usable without turning it into a subscription programme.

## Brand Personality

Plain-spoken, unflattering, warm underneath. The voice of a friend who is good at this and respects you enough to tell you the truth. It does not hype and it does not shame. It never implies you are behind. When the honest answer is two years, it says two years. When the honest answer is that a part of you does not need fixing, it says that too.

The game layer carries the feeling. The writing stays flat and adult.

## Anti-references

Transformation marketing. Before and after photos. Supplement upsell. Streak guilt, loss-framing, and any mechanic that punishes a missed day. Fake urgency and countdown timers. Creator and celebrity physiques as the implied target. The words shred, toned, summer body, alpha, glow-up, looksmaxxing. Spot-reduction claims. BMI as a verdict. Ranking users against each other. Personality typing as identity, including MBTI, Enneagram and somatotypes, none of which have empirical support. Ethnicity as social identity. Ancestry is used here only where physiology has been shown to differ, never as a category that defines a person. Any claim that needs a subscription to be true.

## Design Principles

1. Honesty cuts both ways. Aiming too high and aiming too low are both errors, and the second one is rarely named.
2. The character is a mirror, not a fantasy. If it flatters, the whole product is worthless. The 3D character uses distinct lawful male and female base meshes; female measurements are never applied to male topology.
3. Show the reasoning and the weakest part of it. Never a bare score.
4. It tells you where you stand and the next real thing to do. The weekly checklist is a starting foundation, not a twelve-week progression pretending to know how you recovered.
5. Measurement error is real and photo estimation is not validated. Show zones and bands, never a precise verdict.
6. Adjust for ancestry only where the evidence adjusts, and name the places it does not. A default that silently assumes a European body is a worse error than admitting the data runs out.
7. Safety screening comes before any goal, and that includes screening for disordered eating, not only cardiac risk.
8. Take the motive at the door, then give a better one. People arrive for how they look. The evidence says appearance motivation does not sustain, so the product's job is to hand them a reason that does.
9. Game mechanics may carry progress. They may never manufacture guilt.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG on contrast, focus visibility, semantic structure, screen-reader labels and reduced motion. Then go further.

Works on a slow connection and a cheap Android phone. The game layer must degrade to text without losing the assessment: with WebGL unavailable the 3D never loads, and the verdict, the percentiles and the exercises all still arrive. Every touch target clears 44px, and the controls that move you through the flow sit within thumb reach at the bottom of the screen. No login, no account. Body, safety and goal answers are not stored or transmitted; only the theme and today's checklist ticks may persist locally on the device. No photo ever leaves the device. Readable by someone with low health literacy. Exercise variants support exactly two environments: a home gym with at least adjustable dumbbells, elastic bands with a safe anchor, and other essential kit to be defined by the evidence layer; or a full commercial gym. Zero-equipment and band-only setups are not equivalent modes. Ancestry and country are always optional, always answerable with "prefer not to say", and the tool must work without them. The character creator must represent a wide range of bodies, skin tones and hair without treating any one of them as the default or the goal state.
